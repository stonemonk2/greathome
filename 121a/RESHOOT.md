---

## Rule 1: Landscape. Always.

40 of the 50 existing photos are portrait. Every listing thumbnail, every gallery, every hero image on the web is landscape. Portrait photos get letterboxed with grey bars and read as amateur.

**Turn the phone sideways for every single interior shot.**

The only exceptions: the tall front window, and a vertical detail of the shower column if you want one.

## Rule 2: Keep the phone level

Vertical lines must stay vertical. Tilt the phone down and the walls lean inward; the room looks like it is collapsing.

In the Pixel camera app: **Settings → Grid type → 3x3**, and turn on the **level indicator**. Line up the bubble before every frame. If the floor needs more room, step back — do not tilt.

## Rule 3: Chest height, not eye height

Shoot at roughly **48–55 inches**. Eye level makes floors dominate and ceilings vanish. Chest height is the real-estate standard and makes rooms read larger. A cheap tripod at 4.5 ft is ideal; bracing against a doorframe works.

## Rule 4: 1x lens. Drop to 0.5x only when trapped.

- **1x (≈24mm)** — living room, office/flex, bedroom, exteriors. Default.
- **0.5x ultrawide (≈16mm)** — bathroom and galley kitchen only, where you physically cannot back up further.
- **No fisheye.** Curved walls read as amateur, and overstating room size in a rental listing is a real liability, not just a style problem.

## Rule 5: Shoot from corners

Standing in a doorway shooting straight at the far wall gives you a flat box. Stand in a **corner** so two walls converge — that is what creates depth. Most rooms want two corner shots from opposite ends.

## Pre-shoot punch list

Do all of this **before** the camera comes out. Every item here is something that will otherwise show up in a photo and cost you a prospect.

### Must fix
- [ ] **Peel the Milgard stickers off all 5 windows** — visible in the current set, reads "unfinished"
- [ ] **Cover the open junction box** in the upper kitchen cabinet — safety first, and it is also an RRIP item for the Sept 2 inspection
- [ ] **Close and latch the breaker panel**
- [ ] **Strip the shipping plastic from the fridge shelves**, close both doors
- [ ] Remove every shoe, slipper, tool, cord, ladder, rag, and bucket
- [ ] Pull the blue painter's tape and any plastic sheeting still on walls

### Light — this is where the money is
- [ ] **Put the same color-temperature bulb everywhere.** Right now the warm vanity bulbs fight the cool recessed LEDs and the whites go yellow-green. Standardize on **3000K** through the whole unit — it flatters Swiss Coffee walls and warms the charcoal floor.

- [ ] **Blinds fully raised.** Not slats-open — raised. Closed blinds are why the current photos look like a basement.
- [ ] **Wash the windows, inside and out.** Dirty glass kills a photo more than anything else on this list.

### Surfaces
- [ ] **Apply the granite sealer** (Miracle Sealants 511, already bought). Sealed granite photographs noticeably richer — do this first, it needs 24h to cure.
- [ ] **Install the black acrylic divider** at the sink/fridge gap
- [ ] Wipe every fingerprint off the stainless fridge and hood — they scream in photos
- [ ] Buff the granite dry; water spots read as stains
- [ ] **Vacuum and damp-mop the LVP.** Dust is brutally visible on dark floors
- [ ] Toilet seat and lid **down**

### Outside
- [ ] Power wash the patio, fencing, and front door
- [ ] Clear the weeds along the sidewalk strip and the drainage channel
- [ ] Raise the tree canopy, pull the walnut saplings

## Timing

Shoot **mid-morning, roughly 9–11am**, on an overcast-bright or lightly sunny day. You want soft light, not hard shafts through the blinds.

Walk the unit the evening before and note which windows take direct sun in the morning — shoot those rooms **last**, once the sun has moved off them. A blown-out window is unrecoverable; a slightly dim room is not.

## Staging props

Even a handful of objects breaks up an empty room and gives the eye scale. Cheap, and it makes the Gemini staging pass dramatically better — the model gets real anchors to work from instead of inventing a whole room.

**Worth bringing:**
- A few plants (a snake plant or fiddle leaf reads well against Swiss Coffee)
- A folded throw and two cushions
- A cutting board and a bowl of lemons for the granite
- White towels for the bathroom — rolled, on the vanity
- A small rug for the living room

**Do not** bring anything that implies a fixture you do not have.
